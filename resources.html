Resources

Things I've actually read, listened to, and used — shared honestly, not comprehensively. Everything here is something I've personally found useful, with no affiliate links or sponsorships involved. It's a good starting point, not a definitive list — and if you're in any doubt about your own or your child's situation, please speak to a qualified professional such as your GP, a psychologist, or a psychiatrist.

📖 Books

📗

ADHD 2.0

Edward Hallowell & John Ratey

Updated classic from two leading experts (who both have ADHD themselves). Practical, warm, and genuinely helpful. One of the first books I read after my diagnosis.

Find on Amazon →Click here to read my review →
📗

Driven to Distraction

Edward Hallowell & John Ratey

The original, still essential. Personal stories and clinical insight that help ADHD finally make sense — both to people who have it and people who love them.

Find on Amazon →
📗

The Explosive Child

Ross W. Greene

Technically not an ADHD book, but transformative for any parent whose child has big emotions and difficult behaviour. The Collaborative Problem Solving approach changed how I parent.

Find on Amazon →
📗

How to ADHD

Jessica McCabe

Based on the brilliant YouTube channel of the same name. Practical, friendly, and full of concrete strategies. A great starting point if you're newly diagnosed.

Find on Amazon →
📗

Smart but Stuck

Thomas E. Brown

Particularly good for adults who were high-achieving at school despite ADHD — the 'bright but' story. Helped me understand why diagnosis so often comes late.

Find on Amazon →Click here to read my review →
📗

ADHD: A Hunter in a Farmer's World

Thom Hartmann

The theory is interesting, but really just a nice way for you to view the world of ADHD superpowers.

Find on Amazon →
📗

The PDA Effect: An Autistic Teenager's Guide to Understanding Demand Avoidance

Ethan Knight

Written by an 18-year-old with lived experience of PDA (Pathological/Persistent Demand Avoidance). Technically an autism book rather than an ADHD one, but PDA overlaps enough with ADHD households that it's worth having on the shelf.

Find on Amazon →

🌐 Websites & Online Communities

💻

ADHD UK

adhduk.co.uk

The best UK-specific resource I've found. Includes information on diagnosis, treatment, support, and a forum of people who genuinely understand.

Visit ADHD UK →
🏥

NICE Guidelines on ADHD

Official UK clinical guidance

Dry but important — especially useful if you're advocating for support at school or pushing for assessment on the NHS.

Read the guidelines →
🤝

r/ADHD on Reddit

Community forum

Large, active community of people with ADHD sharing experiences, strategies, and support. Quality varies but there's real warmth here — especially for newly diagnosed adults.
